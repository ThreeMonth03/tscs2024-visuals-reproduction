# Research terms and reading the results

The study title is **Generational Differences in Sharing Visuals by Phone and Preserving Digital Files**. Here, *visuals* means photos and videos. The results compare age groups at one point in time; they do not estimate the effect of belonging to a generation. Personal digital files can also contain other kinds of content.

## Activities and denominators

The survey population is adults aged 18 or older who usually live in private households on Taiwan's main island or in Penghu. Interviews took place in 2024–2025 and included 1,338 respondents.

| Indicator | Meaning | Analysis population |
|---|---|---|
| Taking photos | Frequency of phone photography | Valid frequency responses; national count: 1,280 |
| Recording videos | Frequency of phone video recording | Valid frequency responses; national count: 1,283 |
| Sharing with family and friends | Frequency of sharing self-taken photos or videos by phone | Valid frequency responses; national count: 1,292 |
| Sharing with an unspecified audience | Frequency of sharing self-taken photos or videos with an unspecified audience | Valid frequency responses; national count: 1,292 |
| Sharing experience | Whether respondents have shared with each of the two audiences | Valid frequency responses for both audiences; national count: 1,292 |
| Reporting no file production | Respondents reporting that they do not produce digital files | All valid personal-file responses; national count: 1,336 |
| Primary storage location | The main location selected for personal files | Respondents choosing one of the four specified storage locations; national count: 1,213 |
| Original-device storage | The device where the files were created is the main storage location | The same four-location population |

Each plotted percentage uses the valid respondents **within the displayed group**, with survey weights. Phone ownership uses all 1,338 respondents. Activity-frequency analyses exclude responses indicating no phone or no relevant device function.

*Frequent* means several times a week or more. *Ever shared* includes occasional sharing. The two sharing indicators are separate: a respondent may have shared with both audiences. Photography and video-recording frequency refer to the previous 12 months; the sharing indicators do not specify the same recall period.

## Comparison groups

Age groups are 18–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60–69, and 70+. Education groups are primary school or less, junior high school, senior high or vocational school, and junior college or higher.

The area grouping follows the 2020 township digital-development classification listed in the [reference catalogue](../references/README.md). Digitally mature areas form one group; the other three categories form the non-mature group. Area classification describes local conditions rather than every resident's skills or activities. Within-area results compare age or education groups separately in each area group.

## Sharing and storage terms

An *unspecified audience* includes the setting of social media sharing; the indicator does not identify individual recipients. Sharing-experience groups distinguish sharing with both audiences, only family and friends, only an unspecified audience, and neither audience.

The four primary storage locations are the original device, a personal computer or hard drive, other storage media including printed copies, and cloud storage or services. The indicator records a main location; it does not measure the number or quality of backup copies. Respondents reporting no file production remain part of the separate file-production comparison.

## Reading statistics

A weighted proportion estimates the share of valid respondents in a group after applying survey weights. Sample counts are unweighted. A proportion difference describes how far apart two group proportions are; multiplying a difference on the 0–1 scale by 100 gives percentage points.

Standard errors and confidence intervals describe estimation uncertainty. An unadjusted p-value belongs to an individual test. BY-adjusted p-values account for the fixed set of tests considered together. Aggregate mode recalculates those adjustments from supplied test results. Survey mode reestimates the tests from the user’s input before applying the same fixed correction families.

Sensitivity results compare the main analysis with alternative weighting and sampling-unit deletion. Their interpretation follows the same outcome definition, denominator, and comparison groups as the main result.
